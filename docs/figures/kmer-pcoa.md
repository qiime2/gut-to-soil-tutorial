**Ordination of the averaged kmer Bray-Curtis distances.** Principal Coordinates Analysis (PCoA) scatter plots are a common way to visualize microbiome beta diversity. PCoA embeds the samples so that distances in the scatter plot approximate the distances in the `DistanceMatrix`, and the axes present how much of the total variance each coordinate explains. Each point represents a single sample, and coloring of points based on metadata is commonly used to aid in discovery of patterns. This plot suggests that through the composting process, the samples look less like fecal material and more like food compost in their microbial composition. The orientation of a PCoA axis is arbitrary. Here, axis 1 is drawn with human excrement at the left so the composting series reads left to right. The percentage on each axis is the share of the total variation in the distance matrix that the axis captures. That value is high here because this constructed data set has only six samples and was designed to model a simple transition along the composting time series; in real data with many samples and sources of variation a low percentage is normal. For example, the percent variation explained by Unweighted UniFrac PCoA axis 1 for Meilander et al. (2024) was 11.26% (the interactive Emperor plot is linked from https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.13887456).
